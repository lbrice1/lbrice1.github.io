I work on scientific machine learning for process systems engineering. My research centers on models that combine engineering knowledge with data, and on the software that makes them usable. My aim is to push what models can do while maintaining safety and trust.

In Data and Intelligent Systems at Princeton University, I build machine learning methods and research software with research groups across disciplines. Much of this work concerns training and evaluation workflows that run at scale on GPU clusters. It also concerns scientific software that is reusable, tested, and documented, so that a group can keep building on it.

I have applied these ideas to fuel cells, natural gas processing, and polymer manufacturing. My recent work includes hybrid models that follow physical and qualitative constraints, as well as frameworks for testing data-driven and statistical methods for chemical plants. Earlier, I pursued this work in the Machine Learning, Optimization, and Statistics group at The Dow Chemical Company. There, I also taught in a data science training program for industrial researchers. I coauthored [*AI in Chemical Engineering*](/book/) (CRC Press, 2025), a textbook for students and industry practitioners. It covers unsupervised, supervised, and reinforcement learning, with Python examples and industrial case studies.

I come from Costa Rica, where I earned my undergraduate degree at the Universidad de Costa Rica and began teaching. There, I taught kinetics, thermodynamics, and experimental data analysis to chemical engineering students. I earned my PhD in chemical engineering from Louisiana State University.
